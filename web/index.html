Supported by machine learning

Why it's needed: satellite maps of what lies underground (permafrost, ground temperature) stop at 25° N. Several of the best Mars analogs, such as Antarctica's Dry Valleys and the high Andes, are further south. Models trained where the maps exist fill in the rest, and each one is tested on whole regions it never saw.

Learns from ESA's satellite permafrost map how climate, terrain, soil and rock predict ice.

Physics-informed neural network ensemble

How cold is it underground?

Predicts the temperature profile down to 10 m. Its training loss rejects profiles that break heat conduction, and it says when a place is unlike anything it learned from.
